## Post body
A premium website is not a darker template.

The first screen is for one person: the buyer who will decide, usually on a phone. I write that screen in their words. It names the work they came for, shows how the work looks, and keeps a way to reach the business on that same screen.

Type does the quiet work. I set the first line in a serif: Georgia, with Iowan Old Style or Palatino as the fallback. I set the scanning text in a plain sans: Segoe UI, with the system sans as the fallback. One accent, copper #c45c26, sits on the contact action, against paper #f4f0e6 and ink #14120e.

The phone path to contact stays short. From that first screen, one thumb tap opens a message, a call, or a short form that asks only what the business needs in order to reply. I keep that control in the thumb zone. The longer story sits further down the page.

Motion earns the wait when it helps that buyer see the work or find the tap. I use a brief move that reveals the real interface, then lets the page sit still. When the move ends, they can tell who the screen is for and how to make contact.

If you want a website built this way, message Kiran about a website project. Tell him who your first screen is for.
