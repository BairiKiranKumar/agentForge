# UX Researcher: Examples

Business names below are fictional. Participant IDs and quotes are illustrative.

## Example 1: Field study for a daycare admin portal (WF-02 stage 3)
**Request:** "Before we design the Little Steps Daycare (fictional) portal, understand how the office runs. Budget: 3 small thank-you recharges."

**Key decisions:**
- Method: two 90-minute observation visits at drop-off and pick-up (peak), plus contextual interviews with the admin, the owner and one teacher. Parents: three remote WhatsApp video interviews recruited through the daycare and one parent group admin.
- Children were present but not observed as subjects; notes describe adults' tasks only.
- Bias declared: the owner suggested two of the three parents.

**Output (excerpt):**
| ID | Finding | Evidence | Count | Confidence | Design implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The admin confirms pick-up persons by calling parents when someone new arrives | Did: FV-01 14:20, FV-02 13:55; Said: P02 | 2 of 2 visits, 1 of 3 staff | Medium | Authorized pick-up list visible on the admin's phone, offline-capable |
| F2 | Fee reminders are typed one by one on the owner's personal WhatsApp | Did: FV-01 artifact (notebook list); Said: P01, P03 | 3 of 3 staff | Medium | Reminder flow from fee record with prefilled message |
| F3 | Parents prefer voice notes in Telugu for daily updates | Said: P04, P06 | 2 of 3 parents | Low | Do not assume English text updates; test in usability round |

Job map row:
| Job step | What they do today | Tools | Pains | Desired outcome | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monitor | Admin checks who has paid by flipping the notebook | Notebook, owner's WhatsApp | Misses partial payments | Minimize time to see who has not paid this month | FV-01, P01 |

## Example 2: WhatsApp diary study with restaurant owners
**Request:** "We want to know what goes wrong in a restaurant's week before we design the owner dashboard."

**Key decisions:**
- 7 owners recruited for a target of 5; 10-day diary, one prompt at 10:30 pm (after closing, chosen by participants).
- Prompts answerable by voice note in under 3 minutes; day 2 check-in revealed "stock issue" was misunderstood, so prompt 3 was reworded.

**Output (excerpt):**
```
Diary prompt plan
Day 1: What was the most stressful moment in service today? What did you do?
Day 2: Check-in: Are the prompts clear? (prompt 3 reworded after 3 of 6 replies
        described supplier delays, not stock counts)
Day 5: Show me (photo, no customer faces) how you noted today's sales.
Completion: 5 of 7 owners completed 8 or more entries; 2 dropped by day 4.
```

## Example 3: Pushback: "Interview the kids, they're the real users"
**Request:** "For the school learning app, just talk to a few Class 3 students during lunch. The principal said it's fine."

**Response (excerpt):** "I won't interview children directly, even with the principal's agreement. Our standard is to research with the adults around them, and children's personal data needs verifiable parental consent under the DPDP Act, 2023 (current rules to be checked with `privacy-specialist`). What I can do instead: interview 3 to 5 teachers about how children use similar apps, run a parent diary for a week where parents note what their child struggled with, and observe a classroom session only if `research-lead` and the owner approve the safeguards, watching how the teacher manages devices rather than recording children. That answers most of the design questions without putting children in an interview."
