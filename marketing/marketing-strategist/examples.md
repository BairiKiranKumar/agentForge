# Marketing Strategist: Examples

Business names below are fictional unless stated.

## Example 1: Positioning Kiran's preschool website offer
**Request:** "Write positioning for our admissions website package for preschools in Telangana. We have one live preschool site and 12 enquiry messages from owners."

**Key decisions:**
- Real alternatives from the 12 messages: Instagram page plus WhatsApp (7 mentions), a template site from a relative (3), Justdial listing (2). No owner mentioned a rival agency, so the direct tier is a minor row.
- Category: subcategory "admissions website for preschools" because owners search for a solution to missing parent enquiries, not for "web design".
- The biggest anxiety in the messages was "can I change fees and photos myself"; the proof is a 60-second screen recording of the admin panel, which exists. The second anxiety ("yearly charges") has no proof asset yet; marked missing.
- Seasonal trigger (admissions enquiries peak in early calendar year) marked `[Inference]` until two owners confirm.

**Output (excerpt):**
```
Offer: Preschool admissions website | Version: v1 | Date: 2026-10-21 | Evidence base: 12 enquiries, 1 live build
Best-fit segment: single or two-branch preschools in Telangana towns, active Instagram, no working website, admissions handled on WhatsApp
Anti-segment: chains with an in-house IT team; schools wanting a full ERP in the same budget
Alternatives:
| Tier | Alternative | Why buyers use it | Falls short | Our attribute | Proof |
| Secondary | Instagram + WhatsApp | Free, parents already there | Parents cannot find fees, timings or location in one place; enquiries get lost in chats | Admissions page with enquiry form that sends to WhatsApp | Live site (permission recorded) |
| Do-nothing | Word of mouth | Trusted locally | No reach to new families in the area | Google-findable site + Business Profile setup | Search Console screenshot [pending] |
Core message: Parents find your school, see fees and timings, and enquire on WhatsApp.
| Pillar | Claim | Proof that exists today | Objection answered |
| Found | Shows up when parents search nearby | Proof missing: create after 8 weeks of data | "Nobody searches online here" |
| Clear | Fees, timings, photos and location on one page | Live site walkthrough | "Parents just call anyway" |
| In your control | Update fees and photos yourself | Admin panel recording | "Can I change it myself?" |
Words to use (verbatim): "parents keep asking the same questions", "admission enquiry", "fees details"
Open: yearly cost question has no proof asset; ask Kiran for the maintenance terms to show openly.
```

## Example 2: Channel plan for a cloud kitchen launch with no paid budget
**Request:** "Our client (Spice Route Kitchen, fictional, Hyderabad) opens next month. Give a go-to-market plan. Budget is zero, owner can give 3 hours a week."

**Key decisions:**
- Three hours a week rules out running four social platforms. Channels: Google Business Profile (`local-seo-specialist`), Instagram Reels (owner films, `instagram-specialist` plans), WhatsApp for orders and repeat customers (`whatsapp-business-specialist`).
- Aggregator listings are the real alternative customers use; the plan positions direct WhatsApp ordering for repeat regulars, not as a replacement for aggregators on day one.
- Tier 1 launch, but trimmed: no landing page beyond the menu page that exists.

**Output (excerpt):**
| Phase | Dates | Channel | Job | Asset | Owner | Hours/week | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-launch | Weeks -3 to -1 | Google Business Profile | Discover | Verified profile, menu, 10 real photos | `local-seo-specialist` | 1 | Profile verified |
| Pre-launch | Weeks -2 to 0 | Instagram | Trust | 3 Reels: kitchen, signature dish, owner intro | owner + `instagram-specialist` | 1.5 | 3 Reels ready |
| Launch | Weeks 1 to 4 | WhatsApp | Convert and retain | Click-to-chat link on bag sticker, opt-in line | `whatsapp-business-specialist` | 0.5 | 30 opted-in regulars, else review |

Assumption: delivery radius and aggregator commission are not known; the "direct ordering" pillar is held until the owner confirms they want it.

## Example 3: Pushback on an unprovable claim
**Request:** "Make the core message 'Nizamabad's No. 1 dental clinic'. The owner insists."

**Key decisions:**
- No ranking, award or third-party data supports "No. 1"; a superlative without evidence breaks the claims rule and likely ASCI guidance (verify current code).
- Offer an alternative built on what can be proven.

**Response (excerpt):** "I've left 'No. 1' out of the messaging house because nothing we have proves it, and an unsupported superlative in ads can draw complaints under ASCI rules (I'll have `compliance-research-assistant` confirm the current position). What the evidence does support: 412 Google reviews at 4.8 (screenshot dated 2026-10-20) and same-day appointments on WhatsApp. Proposed core message: 'Same-day dental appointments, trusted by 400+ reviewers in Nizamabad.' If the owner has an award or published ranking, send it and I'll revisit."
